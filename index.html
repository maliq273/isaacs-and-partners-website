Terms of Service

1. Scope, acceptance and service delivery

These terms apply to enquiries and services supplied by Isaacs & Partners (“we”, “us”). A service engagement begins only when we have confirmed availability, agreed the scope and price in writing, completed any required onboarding and received the agreed deposit or advance payment. A quote, invoice, service schedule or signed engagement letter may add matter-specific terms; if there is a conflict, the later signed matter-specific agreement governs to the extent lawful.

We will describe the deliverables, assumptions, estimated timeframes, responsible service provider and any exclusions in the quote or engagement letter. Timeframes are estimates unless expressly guaranteed in writing. We may request documents, instructions or approvals needed to proceed. Delays caused by missing information, third parties, courts, government departments, VFS, SARS, CIPC or other authorities are outside our direct control.

2. Fees, deposits, payment and “no service, no fee”

Fees are payable only for the services and work agreed with the client, subject to any clearly disclosed booking fee, retainer, subscription, reserved-time fee or third-party amount accepted in writing. Our commercial principle is “no service, no fee” for work not performed: we do not charge an unagreed professional fee for a service not supplied. Where a matter is billed by milestone, hourly work, document/page, per payslip or subscription, the quote or service schedule defines when the fee is earned.

Unless a written quote says otherwise, payment for a consultation, hearing, representation or other dated booking is due at least two calendar days before the booking. A booking is not confirmed until availability is confirmed and payment requirements are met. For immigration matters, the engagement letter may require a 50% deposit to open the file and the balance before a VFS/DHA submission or booking. Third-party payments and disbursements must be funded separately where requested.

We will not promise an immigration, SARS, CIPC, employment, legal or court outcome. Fees cover the agreed professional work, not a guaranteed result. No service may be started or billed outside the agreed scope without the client's approval, except urgent steps expressly authorised in writing.

3. Immigration services

We assist with the immigration route expressly listed in the quote, which may include document review, application preparation, submission support, status follow-up, appeals or review-related coordination where accepted. The client must provide complete, truthful and current information and disclose refusals, overstays, criminal matters and material changes. False or withheld information can cause refusal, delays or termination of the engagement.

Decisions remain with the Department of Home Affairs, courts and other competent authorities. Government/VFS fees, SAQA and professional-body assessments, translations, police clearances, medicals, courier charges, counsel and other third-party costs are not included unless expressly itemised. Representation at VFS or Home Affairs is charged at R800 per hour, subject to the agreed booking and scope.

4. HR / IR and employment matters

HR/IR services may include employment documentation, payroll support, disciplinary process administration, chairing, workplace advice and CCMA-related assistance where agreed. The employer remains responsible for employment decisions and for supplying accurate payroll and employee data. We do not guarantee that a disciplinary outcome, dismissal, tax result or dispute outcome will be upheld by a court, bargaining council or tribunal. Representation is provided only where legally permitted and agreed in writing.

5. Business compliance and subscriptions

Business compliance work is limited to the service(s) listed in the signed service schedule. A monthly subscription is billed at the agreed rate and cadence; the included service list, start date, renewal and any minimum term will be disclosed before acceptance. Additional services, historic corrections, penalties, audits, complex remediation and third-party charges are separately quoted. Clients remain responsible for the accuracy of records and for making decisions required of directors, employers or taxpayers.

6. Legal services and professional responsibility

Legal services are provided only within the scope confirmed in writing and by or under the supervision of a suitably qualified and authorised professional where the law requires this. We will identify when a matter requires an attorney, advocate, notary, conveyancer, tax practitioner or other regulated specialist. Where Isaacs & Partners acts as a consultancy or coordinates an external practitioner, the responsible provider and fee arrangement will be disclosed. No attorney-client or other regulated professional relationship should be assumed solely from a website enquiry or an AI conversation; it is established only when confirmed through the appropriate engagement process.

7. Client responsibilities, confidentiality and documents

Clients must provide complete and accurate information, respond to requests in reasonable time, keep contact details current, review drafts and approve filings before submission where requested. We will take reasonable steps to protect confidential information and personal data. Information may be shared with authorities, courts, payment providers or professional advisers only where needed to provide the service, meet a legal obligation or as otherwise lawfully authorised. Clients should not send passwords or payment-card security codes by email or chat.

8. Complaints, liability and statutory rights

Please send complaints to info@isaacsandpartners.online with the matter reference and details. We will acknowledge and review complaints reasonably promptly. Nothing in these terms excludes or limits a right, remedy, duty or liability that cannot lawfully be excluded under South African law, including applicable rights under the Consumer Protection Act, Electronic Communications and Transactions Act, Protection of Personal Information Act and laws governing legal practitioners. Any lawful limitation of liability must be expressly agreed in the matter-specific engagement and cannot apply to fraud, wilful misconduct or liability that law prohibits limiting.

Refund Policy

Refunds for work already started

For consultations, document preparation, applications, drafting, research and other services, fees for work already performed and non-recoverable third-party costs are not refundable merely because a client changes their mind, subject always to statutory cancellation rights and remedies where we have failed to perform, performed defectively or are otherwise required by law to refund. If a matter is cancelled before work starts, we will refund amounts not yet earned, less only any lawful, reasonable and disclosed costs or cancellation charge.

For subscriptions, the invoice and service schedule determine the billing period. If a subscription is cancelled, we will calculate any refund or final amount by reference to the agreed terms, services already supplied, statutory rights and reasonable costs; no term is intended to permit double recovery or an unfair penalty.

Refund processing and provider no-show

Where a refund is due, we will initiate it within seven working days using the original payment method where practicable; bank or payment-provider settlement times may add time. If an Isaacs & Partners representative fails to attend a confirmed paid consultation, hearing or representation booking and no suitable replacement is agreed, the client may choose a full refund of the professional booking fee or a replacement booking at no additional professional fee. External authority, court, VFS, courier and other third-party fees remain subject to the third party's rules. We will provide available payment evidence and reasonable assistance.

Cancellation & Rescheduling Policy

Client cancellation of a dated booking

Clients should cancel or request rescheduling in writing to info@isaacsandpartners.online as soon as possible. Our intended standard cancellation schedule for a confirmed consultation, hearing or representation booking is:

  • At least 2 calendar days before: where work has not started, refund of unused professional fees, less only lawful, reasonable and disclosed non-recoverable costs, or rescheduling by agreement.
  • 1 calendar day before: 75% of the professional booking amount will be refunded within 7 working days; up to 25% may be retained only where reasonable and lawful.
  • On the day of the booking: 50% of the professional booking amount will be refunded within 7 working days; up to 50% may be retained only where reasonable and lawful.

These percentages are not automatic penalties. Any retained amount must be reasonable in the circumstances and consistent with applicable law, taking account of the notice provided, the type of booking, whether the time can be rebooked, work already done and actual costs. We will not apply a cancellation charge where prohibited by law, including applicable statutory protections for cancellation caused by death or hospitalisation. If the law requires a lower charge or a different refund, the law prevails.

Availability must be confirmed before a hearing, consultation or representation date is accepted. We request booking and payment at least two calendar days in advance. If we need to reschedule, we will offer a reasonable alternative date or, if the client does not wish to reschedule, refund the unused professional booking fee.

Privacy & POPIA Notice

Personal information we collect and why

We may collect contact details, identity and passport information, company and employment records, payroll/tax data, immigration documents, payment status, correspondence and information needed to assess or provide a requested service. Depending on the matter, some information may be sensitive. We process it for enquiries, onboarding, service delivery, billing, recordkeeping, legal compliance, security and dispute handling, using an appropriate lawful basis and safeguards under applicable South African data-protection law.

Information is shared only with authorised staff and service providers, payment processors, professional advisers or public authorities where reasonably necessary and lawful. Cross-border processing may occur through approved technology providers; where applicable, we will take steps required by POPIA. We retain information for the period needed for the stated purpose, legal obligations, professional records and dispute resolution, then securely delete or de-identify it where appropriate.

You may request access to, correction of or information about your personal data, or raise a privacy concern, by emailing info@isaacsandpartners.online. Identity verification may be required. Certain lawful grounds may limit deletion or access requests. This notice is a public summary and should be read with our detailed internal privacy, retention and information-officer procedures.

If unresolved, either party may use a competent regulator, statutory complaint channel or court with jurisdiction. Nothing in this policy prevents a consumer from exercising statutory rights or referring a complaint to an applicable regulator, including the Information Regulator for data-protection matters or the Legal Practice Council for matters within its jurisdiction.
